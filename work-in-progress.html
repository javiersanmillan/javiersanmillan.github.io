Work in progress

Housing subsidies and neighbourhood choice
This paper, developed with colleagues from the SEGUE project, analyses how housing subsidies shape neighbourhood selection among low-income households. The results suggest that subsidy design and housing-market constraints can channel recipients towards more disadvantaged neighbourhoods, with implications for the spatial reproduction of inequality.
The geographical scale of segregation in Santiago de Chile and Amsterdam
Together with Marisol Brito-Doerr, I analyse the geographical scale of segregation (from macro-scale patterns to more mosaic-like configurations) in the capital cities of two countries with substantially different housing systems: Chile and the Netherlands. Preliminary results indicate that the measured intensity of residential separation between high- and low-income groups is highly sensitive to the spatial measure employed. They also point to the important role of housing policies in shaping the geographical scale of segregation.
